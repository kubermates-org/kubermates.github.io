  at a better time. Bringing together leaders from federal, state, and local government
  agencies and higher education, panelists focused on how their organizations are
  managing disruption without losing momentum. Across sessions, speakers shared practical
  examples of how agencies are making thoughtful technology decisions to strengthen
  security, improve service delivery, and support mission outcomes in a fast-moving
  environment. Here’s a look at the key takeaways: Data only matters if it helps agencies
  act with greater speed, clarity, and confidence. Good data stewardship is less about
  collecting more information and more about creating the conditions for better decisions—ones
  that are measurable, repeatable, and grounded in mission needs. I sat down with
  Hailaeos “Holly” Troy, senior DevOps automation architect for the City and County
  of Denver, to discuss how a data-driven approach to automation is delivering measurable
  results. By identifying high-value use cases, tracking outcomes, and embedding safeguards
  for sensitive information, Denver is building a scalable model for responsible automation.
  The session reinforced another recurring theme: impact comes from applying data
  with purpose. The strongest agencies are not those with the most tools, but those
  that can turn insight into action while maintaining accountability. As missions
  grow more distributed, agencies are rethinking how their technology environments
  are structured. Rather than treating edge and enterprise systems as separate domains,
  many are working toward a unified, policy-driven architecture.'
