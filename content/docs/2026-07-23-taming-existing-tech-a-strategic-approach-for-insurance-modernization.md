  remains an incredibly data-heavy industry with deep roots. Based on product offerings
  and strict regulatory data retention rules, a significant number of existing systems,
  some decades-old, are still in operation. It''s not uncommon to find COBOL-coded
  applications or AS/400s continuing to run mission-critical workloads. There''s a
  valid reason for this: the insurance sector was an early adopter of these data-heavy
  environments, and the technology worked well. However, managing this data over decades
  created heavy technical debt. For example, liability insurance policies often require
  data retention periods of up to 20 or 30 years beyond a policy''s lifetime. If a
  policyholder lives to be 100 years old, it is possible that mandates would compel
  operational teams to secure and correct 100 to 120-year-old data. We once hoped
  cloud computing would provide a fix for this specific legacy problem, but no single
  technology offers a complete answer. Cloud is a significant component, but virtual
  machines remain a necessity as well. Because of this, IT teams must piece together
  environments or extract data into separate storage layers to keep operations running.
  Today, insurers manage hybrid environments composed of new cloud-driven systems,
  old legacy applications, and a complicated middle layer where operational teams
  try to tie everything together. This infrastructure sprawl can make accelerating
  claims adjudication and other critical business functions incredibly difficult.'
