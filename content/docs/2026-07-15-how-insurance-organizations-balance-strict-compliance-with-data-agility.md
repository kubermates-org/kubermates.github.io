  cloud Security Edge computing Infrastructure Applications Virtualization Share While
  many organizations have recently adopted a cloud-first strategy, a significant number
  of those have since pivoted to operating in an open hybrid cloud environment. For
  insurance organizations, adopting a hybrid cloud strategy was largely because moving
  all operational data to the public cloud environments was impossible due to strict
  regulatory requirements and legal data privacy concerns. Insurers hold a vast amount
  of proprietary, personally identifiable data. In addition to basic financial data,
  insurers need to have sensitive information about policyholders so they can accurately
  assess risk. Consequently, insurance organizations were understandably hesitant
  to migrate all their data into the public cloud. Adopting an open hybrid cloud environment
  offers a pragmatic path forward. It allows organizations to mitigate data risk by
  keeping sensitive workloads under direct control while selectively taking advantage
  of public cloud scalability—a balance that helps reduce technical debt and maintain
  compliance without sacrificing operational agility. Operating in a hybrid IT environment
  requires enterprise-grade consistency. For predictable performance, decision-making
  applications must receive accurate data precisely when it''s needed. For intelligent
  models parsing complex claims, data quality must remain uniform across sources,
  even when specific underlying requirements vary. Here are some examples of how data
  can vary and the level of precision insurers require: An insurance company must
  know with 100% certainty, at any exact second, whether a policyholder is alive or
  dead. There is absolutely zero room for lag, typos, or guesswork.'
