summary: Why VM and container platforms ended up separate What KubeVirt changed about
  running VMs and containers together What VM and container consolidation means for
  your roadmap Two platforms, two teams, two procurement relationships, all doing
  one job. There’s a reason it ended up this way. There isn’t a reason it has to stay
  this way. Ask anyone at a typical enterprise why the VM platform and the container
  platform are separate, and they’ll give you a sensible answer. The VM estate has
  been there for fifteen years. It runs the workloads the business depends on. Kubernetes
  got stood up later, when application teams started building microservices, and giving
  them their own environment made more sense than retrofitting one onto VMware. Two
  platforms, two teams, two roadmaps. That’s how most enterprises got here. The reasoning
  was sound at the time. The question is whether it still is. This is the consolidation
  question most enterprises haven’t actually revisited, and it’s the one quietly absorbing
  more of your budget each year.
