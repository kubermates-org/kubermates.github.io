  has a compute problem. Not a shortage of ideas, but a shortage of infrastructure
  that can keep up with them. It also has a platform problem—how can we deliver generative
  AI (gen AI) capabilities through the right architecture and operating model so institutions
  can provide access without handing every user unrestricted access to expensive models
  and runaway spend. Small language models (SLMs) are usually domain-specific AI models
  developed with significantly fewer parameters than their massive counterparts, typically
  ranging from 1 billion to 10 billion parameters. While large language models (LLMs)
  function like vast, general-purpose libraries, SLMs are specialized models trained
  on high-quality, curated datasets to excel at specific tasks. This reduced scale
  allows them to run efficiently on local devices or edge infrastructure, offering
  a cost-effective way to improve data privacy and reduce latency. By focusing on
  narrow domains rather than the entire internet, these models provide a strategic
  pathway for enterprises to deploy gen AI without the massive computational overhead
  of traditional models. The modern research workflow spans an enormous range of compute
  demands. Training a domain-specific language model on clinical data looks nothing
  like running a molecular dynamics simulation, which looks nothing like serving a
  fine-tuned model to a team of researchers in real time.'
